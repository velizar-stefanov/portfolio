Meraki Templates

Design challenges from working on Meraki Templates, a digital shop selling templates across various niches.

Meraki Templates is an Etsy shop selling digital templates across a variety of niches that I co-founded along with my sister. We worked together on tasks such as generating copy for the products, designing them, researching for SEO, and more. Our work was organized into professional fields, aesthetics, and media formats such as website, carousel, presentation templates, and others.

Canva website templates

Website templates are a great way for non-designers to get a site up and running without investing much time or money.

Templates are highly customizable and easy to use, but these qualities are what make designing them challenging. They need to be straightforward to rearrange and shorten by deleting parts of the structure. Mobile layouts also have to be considered, because Canva generates them automatically and they often break.

We achieved this flexibility by splitting the design into sections that can be swapped, duplicated, or deleted. Designing with mobile in mind meant re-checking for strange layouts every time we tried something daring. Although tedious to do, we had to stick to simple structures and constantly group elements together to keep the mobile version relatively stable for users.

Carousel & single post templates

Marketing or hobby-posting on social media platforms can be hard whenever something more complicated than a photo needs to be posted. Especially if the design is made from scratch and is chasing a certain aesthetic. This is why templates for posts are so popular, especially the free ones.

Making these templates, however, is difficult as usual. Where text is necessary, it must be big enough to be easily read from a mobile device. However, it also has to adapt to various lengths of copy. Introducing interesting designs is sometimes off the table because of the need for text flexibility. This means playing around with typography—which is a major tool in a designer's toolkit, is very limited.

The solution to these problems is to consider whether text sizes have to be smaller or bigger, how certain designs might affect usability, and how voluminous copy might modify the design.

Slide deck templates

We made six decks to help social media managers present their work to clients: a pre-call questionnaire, a business proposal, an onboarding process, monthly and yearly analyses, and a social media strategy.

Some of the presentations covered a variety of metrics, each needing its own representation: current follower count, audience demographics, posting schedule, and more. Getting this right mattered because the templates needed to be easily digestible. Using a table for everything wouldn't have been appropriate.

We took the time to evaluate which method of displaying information best fit each metric. For some, tables were appropriate; for others, line, bar, or even pie charts were better suited. To keep slides easy to scan, we used generous amounts of white space and a clear layout.

Takeaway

Working on Meraki Templates significantly improved my ability to consider the user and their needs throughout the design process. I also learned to be adaptable in design and explore creative solutions to work around restrictions. These limitations came from the software we used to make templates shareable, Canva, and the necessity for templates to be user-friendly.
